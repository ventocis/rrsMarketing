# How to Submit Your Defensive Driving Certificate to the Court

_Last updated: September 2, 2026_

**Texas, short version:** download the court copy of your certificate the moment you finish the course, then deliver it to the court that granted the course, by portal upload, e-file, email, in person, or mail, whichever that court accepts, together with your Type 3A driving record if the court requires one, before the court's deadline. The court then dismisses the charge. Everything below is the detail, Texas first, then other states.

---

## Texas: what the court needs from you

Once a Texas court has approved you for a driving safety course, it wants three things back:

1. **The court copy of your uniform certificate of completion.** Your certificate PDF has two pages: a court copy and an insurance copy. Submit the court copy. Your certificate number is in the format `CP1234-########`; the court validates it in TDLR's system.
2. **Your Type 3A certified driving record from Texas DPS**, if the court requires you to supply it (most do). Order it online from DPS for $12 as an instant PDF. It has to be the Type 3A, not the free abstract. [How to order the right one](/texas/type-3a-driving-record).
3. **Any form the court itself requires**, most commonly a sworn affidavit that you have not completed a driving safety course for dismissal in the past 12 months. [Which courts require a notarized affidavit](/texas/notarized-affidavit).

You do **not** need to send anything to TDLR or DPS. The provider reports your completion to TDLR; you report it to the court.

---

## Texas: how to deliver it

Use the method your court accepts. Larger courts and most county justice courts now take uploads; some small municipal courts still want paper.

1. **Court portal or e-file.** Upload the certificate PDF (and the Type 3A) and enter your case or citation number exactly as printed. Some courts, including Harris County's justice courts and Dallas County JP 1-1, accept nothing else. Save the confirmation.
2. **Email.** Attach the PDF and put your full name, date of birth, citation number and the court name in the body. Keep the sent email.
3. **In person.** Bring a printed court copy, the Type 3A, your ID, and any affidavit. Ask the clerk for a stamped receipt.
4. **Mail.** Print the court copy, write the citation number on it, include the Type 3A and affidavit, and use tracking. Postmark dates matter at some courts.

Your court's accepted method, address and forms are in the [Texas court directory](/texas/courts).

## Texas: deadlines and the five-day lag

- **The statutory window is 90 days** from the date the court granted the course (Code of Criminal Procedure art. 45A.351). Courts can extend it for good cause if you ask before it runs out.
- **Courts set their own paperwork deadlines** inside that window. In our verified data they run from [10 days to 180 days](/texas/dsc-deadlines-by-court). The letter or email the court sent when it approved you is the deadline that counts.
- **Leave a cushion.** TDLR notes a new certificate "may take up to 5 days after the issuance date to appear" in its validation system. A clerk who checks the day you finish may not see it yet. Finish the course at least a week before the court's deadline.
- **Confirm it landed.** Check the court's portal or call the clerk two to three business days after submitting. A dismissal is not final until the court enters it.

## Texas: if the court rejects the certificate

The usual reasons are a name that does not match the license or citation, a missing Type 3A, the wrong record type, a missing affidavit, or a certificate for a course the court never approved. Most are fixable within the window. [What to do when a certificate is rejected](/texas/certificate-rejected). If the name on the certificate is wrong, contact us before resubmitting; we correct it and reissue.

## Texas: the insurance copy

The second page of your certificate is for your insurer. Texas insurers typically offer a defensive driving discount for three years on presentation of the certificate; it is separate from the court and optional. [How the insurance discount works](/texas/insurance-discount).

## Other states

**Partner courses (TicketSchool).** Some of our courses outside Texas are delivered by our partner TicketSchool. In states with electronic reporting, TicketSchool submits your completion automatically and you do not need to upload anything; your certificate status updates on its own. If you also need a copy for your records, sign in to TicketSchool and download the PDF, or use the link they emailed.

**Florida.** Many Florida clerks accept electronic reporting for Basic Driver Improvement completed with TicketSchool. If your clerk still asks you to submit, upload or deliver the PDF certificate directly to the clerk by the posted deadline. TLSAE and ADI requirements vary by county and purpose; follow your clerk or DMV instructions.

**Everywhere else.** Check your court or insurer's instructions for "traffic school" or "defensive driving" submission steps and a deadline, often within 30 days of your election or judgment. Electronic reporting is typically instant to two business days; clerk systems can take longer to reflect it.

Acceptance and timing vary by court and insurer. Always confirm your local instructions.

## Troubleshooting

**Didn't receive the PDF?** Check spam for your completion email, or download it again from your course dashboard. For partner courses, sign in at TicketSchool.

**Name or citation mismatch.** Your profile must match your ID and ticket exactly (spelling, middle initials, suffix). Contact support before resubmitting and we will correct and reissue.

**Clerk can't find my completion.** If it was reported electronically, wait one to two business days and call again. Otherwise resend the PDF with your case number in the subject line and file name.
